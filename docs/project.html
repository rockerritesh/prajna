01 — The everyday problem

Your meter shows one number for everything

A home electricity meter tells you the total being used right now — but not what's using it. Is the bill high because of the fridge, the water heater, or that old AC? Normally you'd have to put a sensor on every single appliance to find out. That's expensive and impractical.

This project takes the smarter route, called Non-Intrusive Load Monitoring (NILM): measure only the one main wire, then let software figure out the breakdown. The hardware stays tiny; the intelligence lives in the model.

03 — The core idea

Splitting one signal back into appliances

Every appliance leaves a "fingerprint" in the total power: a fridge cycles on and off, a kettle is a short tall spike, an AC is a long heavy block. The model learns these fingerprints and pulls the single line apart into who's-using-what — this is the heart of the project.

07 — Hardware in detail

Exact parts, and how they wire together

Everything below is cheap, sold in Kathmandu electronics shops, and beginner-friendly. The core build is just three things: the clamp, a small bias circuit, and the ESP32. The voltage sensor and SD card are optional add-ons.

SCT-013-030 current clamp

Clips around one insulated wire and outputs a small AC voltage (0–1 V). Core sensor.

qty 1 · core

ESP32 DevKit board

Reads the sensor on its ADC pins and logs over USB or Wi-Fi. The brain of the rig.

qty 1 · core

2 × 10 kΩ resistors

Form a divider that lifts the sensor signal to mid-voltage so the ADC can read it.

qty 2 · core

10 µF capacitor

Steadies that mid-voltage reference point so the readings stay clean.

qty 1 · core

Breadboard + jumper wires

Holds the bias circuit and connects everything without soldering.

qty 1 set · core

ZMPT101B voltage module

Add only if you want true power in watts (not just current). Has its own bias + trimmer.

optional

microSD card module

For logging offline to a card instead of streaming to a laptop. Connects over SPI.

optional

USB cable / 5 V supply

Powers the ESP32 and carries the data stream to your computer.

qty 1 · core

Wiring diagram

Connection list (the core build)

Current sensing

  • 3V3 → 10 kΩ → mid-point → 10 kΩ → GND
  • 10 µF capacitor across mid-point and GND
  • Clamp lead 1 → mid-point (bias)
  • Clamp lead 2 → ESP32 GPIO34 (ADC)

Voltage (optional) & power

  • ZMPT101B: VCC → 3V3, GND → GND
  • ZMPT101B: OUT → ESP32 GPIO35 (ADC)
  • ESP32: powered & read over USB to laptop
  • microSD (optional): CS 5, SCK 18, MOSI 23, MISO 19
Before you wire it: the clamp signal must be biased to mid-rail (the 2×10 kΩ + capacitor) or the ESP32's ADC can't read the negative half of the wave. Calibrate the readings against a known load (e.g. a labelled bulb), and set anything near mains up with your supervisor's guidance. The ESP32 ADC is non-linear at the extremes, so keep the signal comfortably within 0–3.3 V.
